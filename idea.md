HalalPilot: an agent that gets small food businesses halal-certified

The neglected problem: For a home-based sambal maker, bakery, or frozen food SME in Malaysia, halal certification is the gate to supermarkets, hotel supply, and export. Most never get it because the paperwork is brutal, not because their product fails. They must trace every ingredient back to a supplier with a valid halal cert, document their processes, and survive an audit. Consultants charge thousands of ringgit, so micro-SMEs give up or stay stuck selling at pasar and on Facebook.

The one-prompt goal: The owner drops in a messy folder (recipes, supplier invoices, kitchen photos) and types “Get me ready for halal certification.” The agent returns a complete, submission-ready application pack.

The 3 WOW features
Ingredient X-ray. The agent breaks every product into its ingredients, matches each to a supplier from the invoices, and runs parallel sub-agents to check each supplier’s halal certificate and expiry date. It flags hidden risk ingredients (gelatin, emulsifiers, flavourings, shortening) and suggests certified substitute suppliers for anything that fails.
Mock auditor showdown. A second agent plays a strict halal auditor and attacks the application pack: missing certs, cross-contamination gaps, weak procedures. The builder agent fixes each finding, and they loop until the pack passes, producing a readiness score. Running this live on stage shows collaboration and validation, which the judges list explicitly.
Certificate watchdog. After submission, the agent keeps watching. When a supplier’s halal cert is about to expire, or the owner adds a new ingredient, it re-checks compliance, drafts the message to the supplier asking for the renewed cert, and updates the application pack. This matters because one expired supplier cert can put the whole certification at risk.
